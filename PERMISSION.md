# Requesting Written Authorization

This document explains how to request permission. It does not grant any right or license.

Except for separately licensed third-party material, the repository's original material may be used only after the copyright holder has issued prior express written authorization covering the intended activity.

## Submit a Request

Use the repository's [permission-request issue form](https://github.com/zhouxxx123123/create-every-baseline-v2/issues/new?template=permission-request.yml), or contact the copyright holder through the [zhouxxx123123 GitHub profile](https://github.com/zhouxxx123123).

Do not post confidential information, personal identification numbers, credentials, customer data, or trade secrets in a public GitHub issue.

Include:

1. Your full legal name and, if applicable, organization name.
2. A reliable contact method.
3. The exact files, Skill directories, version, tag, or commit SHA requested.
4. The intended purpose and whether it is commercial, internal, educational, research, or another category.
5. Whether you need to run, modify, distribute, host, integrate, publish, sublicense, or create derivative works.
6. Expected users, deployment model, distribution channel, territory, and duration.
7. Any requested support, warranty, maintenance, exclusivity, or attribution terms.

## When Permission Exists

Permission exists only after the copyright holder issues a completed written authorization that expressly identifies the recipient and granted rights. Submitting a request, receiving repository access, opening an issue, receiving an acknowledgment, or making payment does not itself grant permission.

The copyright holder may approve, reject, limit, revoke, charge for, or impose conditions on a request, subject to any signed agreement and applicable law.

A reusable starting form is available in [AUTHORIZATION_TEMPLATE.md](AUTHORIZATION_TEMPLATE.md). The blank template is not valid authorization and should be reviewed by qualified counsel before material commercial use.
